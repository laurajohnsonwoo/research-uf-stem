# The University of Florida STEM Study Abroad Map
### Prestige, Research Placements, and Where the Signal Actually Is — A Companion to the UF STEM Research Map

*Compiled 16 August 2026. Every substantive claim carries a source link. A "Verification Flags" section at the end lists everything that could not be confirmed — read it before acting on any single fact.*

---

## 0. How to Read This Document

UF runs **over 100 reciprocal exchange agreements worldwide** ([internationalcenter.ufl.edu](https://internationalcenter.ufl.edu/sas/academics/program-types/)) plus dozens of faculty-led programs, and the College of Engineering alone sent **387 students abroad in 2023–24** — third-highest of UF's sixteen colleges ([news.ufl.edu](https://news.ufl.edu/2024/07/engineering-study-abroad)). None of that tells you where to go.

This document applies the same lens as the [research map](UF-STEM-Research-Map.md) and the [clubs map](UF-STEM-Clubs-Map.md): separate what is **genuinely prestigious for a STEM career** from what is **a good experience that will not change your trajectory**.

**The central argument, stated plainly:**

> **UF's study-abroad marketing and UF's study-abroad prestige point in opposite directions.**
> The programs UF promotes hardest are the short faculty-led summer courses — *UF in London*, *UF in Iceland*, *UF in Italy*. They are well-run, easy to administer, and easy to fill. They are also, for a STEM career, close to neutral: three to six weeks of coursework with other UF students, taught by a UF professor, in a nicer place.
> The prestige is somewhere quieter. It is in the **exchange list**, which includes KAIST, POSTECH, KU Leuven, DTU, Politecnico di Milano, ISAE-SUPAERO, the Technion and NTU Singapore — institutions that are, in their specific fields, meaningfully stronger than UF. And it is in the **research placements**, which put you in a European or Asian lab doing work, not coursework.
> Nobody markets those as hard, because they are harder to administer and harder to fill. That is exactly why they are under-applied-to.

**The second argument, which matters more than any single program:**

There is one elite fellowship built specifically for STEM students, it sends you to Cambridge with everything paid, and **UF is permitted to endorse only two candidates for it per year.** That is the Churchill Scholarship, and its campus deadline is **2 October 2026**. Two slots at a university of 55,000 students is an extraordinary ratio, and most UF STEM students have never heard of it.

**Two structural facts about this calendar.** First, the elite graduate fellowships cluster in **August–October of your senior year**, which means the preparation happens in your *junior* summer — earlier than almost anyone expects. Second, the undergraduate research-abroad programs cluster in **October–February**, the same window as the domestic REUs in the research map. You can and should apply to both.

### Tier 3: Where the Marketing Runs Ahead of the Signal

- **The short faculty-led summer and winter programs.** *UF in Antarctica*, *UF in Iceland: Renewable Energy*, *UF in London: Innovating for Access*, *UF in Munich*, *UF in Scandinavia*, *UF in Panama*, *UF in Nepal*, *UF in Greece*, *UF in Cayman Islands*, and roughly a dozen more. These are **genuinely good experiences** and they are the right choice for a lot of people — they are affordable, low-risk, credit-bearing, and they get students abroad who would otherwise never go. **But be honest about what they are:** UF coursework, taught by UF faculty, to a cohort of UF students, in a different country. They do not put you in a foreign institution's research culture, they do not produce a foreign letter of reference, and no graduate committee reads *UF in Iceland* as a signal about your science. Do one if you want to. Do not do one *instead of* an exchange or a research placement and think you have done the same thing.
- **Universities of applied sciences.** Hamburg University of Applied Sciences, Munich University of Applied Sciences, and Zurich University of Applied Sciences (ZHAW) appear on UF's exchange list. German and Swiss *Fachhochschulen* are excellent at practice-oriented engineering teaching and often have superb industry links — but they are structurally **not research universities**, and they do not carry the name recognition of RWTH Aachen or ETH with a graduate admissions committee. This is a real distinction, not a slight. Choose them for the internship pipeline, not for the prestige.
- **"Study abroad" framed as a résumé line.** The most common failure mode. A semester at KAIST doing real coursework in a foreign system is a story. A three-week trip is a photograph.

## 3. The Documented Chain — How This Actually Pays Off

The strongest single piece of evidence in this document is not a program. It is a person.

**Maddie Ross** — a biochemistry student in CLAS — became **UF's 10th Gates Cambridge Scholar** in February 2024, going on to a **Ph.D. in chemistry at the University of Cambridge**, with research interests in Type 1 diabetes at the molecular level ([studentsuccess.ufl.edu](https://studentsuccess.ufl.edu/success-stories/2024/ufs-10th-gates-cambridge-scholar/)).

She did her undergraduate research under **Brent Sumerlin**, principal investigator in the George & Josephine Butler Polymer Research Laboratory — who the [research map](UF-STEM-Research-Map.md) identifies (§3.11) as one of **two genuinely world-class sub-niches inside UF's Tier 2 chemistry department.**

**Read the chain:**

> Identify a world-class pocket → get into that specific lab → the lab produces the work and the letter → the letter carries an elite fellowship → the fellowship carries you to Cambridge.

That is the entire thesis of the research map, executed end to end, with names attached. It is also worth noting what she said about the mechanics: *"I have learned to articulate my thoughts well… Mrs. Medley and the mock interviews really helped."* **Kelly J. Medley** oversees UF's Office of Prestigious Awards, and the mock interviews are a real, free, under-used resource.

**And the honest counterweight:** Ross was UF's **first Gates Cambridge Scholar since 2017** — a seven-year gap. Ten scholars total is thin for an institution of UF's size and ambition. The infrastructure exists and works; almost nobody uses it. That is the opportunity and the indictment in the same sentence.

## 4. The Ladder

Each rung builds on the one below. The elite fellowships at the top are not reachable without the rungs beneath them, and every one of those rungs has an earlier deadline than students expect.

1. **Year 1–2 — Get funded to go at all.**
   **Gilman** (up to $5,000, plus a **$1,000 STEM supplemental award** and up to $3,000 for critical languages) is the entry point if you are Pell-eligible. **About 30% of UF applicants are awarded** — a far better rate than most national scholarships. Contact sas@ufic.ufl.edu.
   Stack it with **HWCOE Study Abroad Scholarships** ($1,000–$2,000, deadlines 15 Oct and 15 Feb), the **Kunde Scholarship** (three $2,000 awards for summer programs in Europe of at least six weeks, financial need considered), **UFIC Summer and Summer Merit Scholarships**, **Wentworth Honors Scholarships**, and **Learning without Borders** for Pell recipients.

2. **Year 2 — Build the domestic research base.**
   None of the elite research-abroad programs will take you without it. **SUIRP explicitly requires at least one year of prior UF research** and a **self-secured placement**, which means you need a PI who will make an introduction. The research map's tactical playbook (§5) is the prerequisite, not a parallel track.

3. **Year 2–3 — Go abroad for research, not for coursework.**
   **DAAD RISE** is the best-designed option in this document: STEM-only, three months, €992/month, matched into an actual German research group, applications 15 Oct – 30 Nov. **Amgen Scholars** at ETH, Cambridge, Karolinska, Pasteur, LMU, Kyoto, NUS, Tsinghua or Tokyo is the most prestigious. **UF in Aachen** is the easiest to get into of the elite-host options because it runs through UF. **SUIRP** funds whatever you can arrange yourself.

4. **Year 3 — Take a semester at a genuinely better institution.**
   You pay UF tuition and keep your financial aid — the exchange model means **tuition at the partner university is waived**. A semester at KAIST, KU Leuven, DTU, Politecnico di Milano or ISAE-SUPAERO is the single highest prestige-per-dollar move available to a UF undergraduate. Note the term restrictions in §2: several of the best ones are **Spring-only**, which means deciding a year ahead.

5. **Year 3 summer — Fulbright preparation.**
   The campus deadline is **early September of senior year**, so the essays and recommenders are a *junior summer* project. There is no version of this that works if you start in August.

6. **Year 4 — The elite awards.**
   **Churchill** (2 Oct, STEM-specific, two UF endorsements available), **Rhodes and Marshall** (14 Aug — before senior year technically begins), **Gates Cambridge**, **Schwarzman** at Tsinghua, **McCall MacBain** at McGill. Use the Office of Prestigious Awards mock interviews.

| Route | Graduate leverage | Professional leverage |

| **Churchill / Gates / Rhodes / Marshall** | Categorically transformative. ## 6. The Mechanics Nobody Explains

**How UF exchange actually works financially.** On a UF Exchange you **pay UF tuition** and the partner university's tuition is **waived**. Financial aid and scholarships continue to apply. This is the single most under-appreciated fact in this document: a semester at a top-30 world university can cost roughly what a semester in Gainesville costs. The barrier is administrative and psychological, not financial.

**Three program types, three different credit models** ([internationalcenter.ufl.edu](https://internationalcenter.ufl.edu/sas/academics/program-types/)):
- **UF Exchange** — reciprocal, UF tuition, **UF credit**, and you are typically the only UF student there. That isolation is the point; it is also why fewer people do it.
- **UF Sponsored** (faculty-led or partner-led) — fees billed through the UF International Center, **UF credit**, travelling as a cohort.
- **Non-UF** (providers, other US universities, direct enrollment) — **transfer credit**, paid directly to the administering organisation.

**Contacts worth having:**
- **Office of Prestigious Awards** — Kelly J. Medley oversees it. Mock interviews are free and were explicitly credited by UF's most recent Gates Cambridge Scholar.
- **CLAS exchanges** — Dr. Brian Harfe, bharfe@ufl.edu. The **Brian and Kate Harfe International Exchange Award** provides up to $400 for students with demonstrated financial need.
- **Study Abroad Services** — sas@ufic.ufl.edu, for Gilman and general advising.
- **HWCOE international programs** — for the engineering exchange list and the $1,000–$2,000 scholarships.

**A hard scheduling note.** Several of the best exchange destinations are **Spring-only** — KU Leuven, DTU (year/fall/spring but check), Politecnico di Milano, Technion, ISAE-SUPAERO (spring/summer), Chalmers, Lund, Twente, Leeds, Sheffield. Spring placement means applying roughly a **year** in advance and sequencing your major requirements around a semester you will not be on campus. Talk to your academic advisor in your **second year**, not your third.

## 7. Verification Flags — Read Before Acting

**Confirmed against primary sources:** UF's 100+ reciprocal exchange agreements · the full HWCOE exchange partner table including KAIST, POSTECH, KU Leuven, DTU, Politecnico di Milano, ISAE-SUPAERO, Technion, NTU, RWTH Aachen, Chalmers, Lund, Tohoku, Twente · the full CLAS/Beyond120 exchange list including UCL, Melbourne, Sydney, UNSW, Monash, NTU, Manchester, Glasgow, Bristol · the UF-sponsored engineering program list · UF in Aachen (10 weeks, 6 UF credits, €2,000 RWTH scholarship) · NSF IRSC at Seoul National (6 US students) · Newcastle 6-week research internship · SUIRP (8–10 weeks, ≥1 year prior research, self-secured placement) · Churchill campus deadline 2 Oct 2026 and the two-endorsement limit · Fulbright campus deadline 4 Sept 2026 · Rhodes and Marshall campus deadline 14 Aug 2026 · Truman 11 Dec 2026 · Boren campus deadline 9 Jan 2027 · Beinecke 20 Feb 2026 and its one-endorsement limit · HWCOE scholarships $1,000–$2,000 with 15 Oct and 15 Feb deadlines · Kunde three $2,000 awards · exchange tuition waiver model · DAAD RISE eligibility, €992/month, and the 15 Oct – 30 Nov 2026 window · Amgen Scholars host institutions and ~10 per European site · Maddie Ross as UF's 10th Gates Cambridge Scholar, first since 2017, mentored by Brent Sumerlin.

**Could not be confirmed — verify before relying on it:**

1. **Whether Rhodes and Marshall campus endorsement is genuinely closed.** The published date is 14 August 2026, two days before this document. Deadlines occasionally move and offices occasionally make exceptions. **Email the Office of Prestigious Awards rather than assuming.**
2. **The Gates Cambridge campus deadline is listed as "TBD for 2026"** on UF's own page. So is **McCall MacBain**. Check directly.
3. **UFIC's own program-search and exchange-listing pages returned 404s** during this research, and the STEM Study Abroad Fair page is dead. The exchange lists here come from the **college** pages (HWCOE and CLAS/Beyond120), which may not be exhaustive or perfectly current.
4. **No GPA thresholds, class-standing requirements, or language prerequisites were published** for either exchange list. Several destinations note language limitations informally — Universidad de los Andes is Spanish-only, Université de Technologie de Troyes has "limited English" — but there is no published general policy.
5. **Exchange application deadlines are not published** on the pages consulted. Given that many destinations are Spring-only, this is the most important missing number in the document. Ask UFIC directly.
6. **Cohort sizes, stipends and eligibility for the Newcastle research internship** were not published. Same for NSF IRSC beyond the six-student figure.
7. **UF's record with Churchill, Rhodes and Marshall could not be established.** Gates Cambridge is documented at 10 scholars. For the others, no UF count surfaced — which may mean the record is thin, or simply that it is not published. Ask the Office of Prestigious Awards for the actual numbers before assuming either.
8. **Amgen Scholars 2027 application dates** are "coming November 1 for all other regions" per the program; only Australia was open at the time of research. Treat 1 November as the date to check, not the date to apply.
9. **Whether UF students have ever been placed via DAAD RISE or Amgen Scholars** is unknown. Both are open to any US undergraduate; neither publishes institutional breakdowns.
10. **The Gilman ~30% UF award rate** is UF's own published figure. Plausible and consistent with Gilman's national profile, but self-reported.
11. **Institutional prestige rankings in §2 are my assessment**, drawn from general standing in each field rather than from a single ranking table. Reasonable people would move two or three entries between tiers. The distinction between research universities and universities of applied sciences is structural and not a matter of opinion; the ordering within Tier 1 is.
12. **Everything in §5 is inference.** No outcome data exists for UF study-abroad participants. Named employers are illustrative of a category, not placement records.

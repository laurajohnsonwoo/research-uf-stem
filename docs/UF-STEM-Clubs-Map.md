# The University of Florida STEM Clubs & Groups Map
### Teams, Chapters, and Where the Real Signal Is — A Companion to the UF STEM Research Map

*Compiled 16 August 2026. Every substantive claim carries a source link. A "Verification Flags" section at the end lists everything that could not be confirmed — read it before acting on any single fact.*

---

## 0. How to Read This Document

UF has **1,000+ registered student organizations** on [GatorConnect](https://orgs.studentinvolvement.ufl.edu/organizations). Roughly a hundred of them are STEM-adjacent. That number is not useful information for deciding where to spend your evenings for four years.

This document applies the same lens as the [research map](UF-STEM-Research-Map.md): separate the things that are **verifiably excellent** from the things that are **fine but ordinary** from the things that are **mostly a logo and a GroupMe**.

The single most important thing to understand up front:

> **A club and a lab are not competing for the same slot in your life. They do different jobs.**
> A lab gives you a letter-writer, a technique, and a publication. A club gives you a team, a deadline you cannot renegotiate, a physical artifact, and — at the top tier — a national result that shows up in a search of your name. The students who do best have one of each. The failure mode is having four of one and none of the other.

There is one asymmetry worth knowing before you read further. In the research map, UF's elite pockets were **narrow and specialised** — eight buildings out of sixteen colleges. In clubs, the picture is different: UF's competition teams are **broadly strong and, in civil engineering, nationally dominant in a way no UF research department is.** The Eckhoff Steel Bridge Team has won five consecutive national titles. No UF academic department has an equivalent claim.

**The UF Fall 2026 Student Organization Fair is 27 August 2026, 4:00pm, at the Stephen C. O'Connell Center** ([oconnellcenter.ufl.edu](https://www.oconnellcenter.ufl.edu/events/uf-fall-2026-student-organization-fair/)). That is **eleven days from this document's date.**

This is the highest-leverage two hours available to a new student, and it is worth preparing for rather than wandering into:

1. **Pick six targets from this document before you go.** The fair is loud, crowded, and optimised for the organisations with the best table displays — which is not the same set as the organisations with the best outcomes.

Miss the window and you generally wait a full term — the same dynamic the research map documented for labs.

If you are reading this after 27 August: most technical teams still take members through the first few weeks of the semester, and all of them take members again in January. Email the address in this document rather than waiting for the spring fair.

## 3. Deep Dives — Tier 1

### 3.1 ASCE Steel Bridge & Concrete Canoe — *UF's most dominant undergraduate program, full stop*

This is the strongest claim in this document and it rests on the hardest evidence available: **repeat national championships in a competition with a large, serious field.**

**Steel Bridge (the Eckhoff team):** captured its **fifth consecutive national title** in 2025 ([eng.ufl.edu](https://www.eng.ufl.edu/news/engineering-education/uf-concrete-canoe-team-claims-its-fifth-championship/)). Five in a row is a dynasty, not a good year.

**Concrete Canoe:** won **first overall at the 38th annual ASCE Concrete Canoe Competition** at California Polytechnic State University, San Luis Obispo, narrowly beating Virginia Tech. That is UF's **fifth national title, four of them in the last six years**. Earlier form: first overall in 2019 with 92.3/100, the highest point total in UF history at the time, winning both Oral Presentation and Design Paper ([essie.ufl.edu](https://essie.ufl.edu/concrete-canoe-and-steel-bridge-place-first-and-second-in-national-competitions/)).

**The people:**
- **Taylor Rawlinson, Ph.D.** — faculty adviser and Director of the Weil Hall Structures & Materials Laboratory. A faculty adviser who also runs the lab you build in is a materially different arrangement from one who signs forms.
- **2025 co-project managers:** Brennan Kade (then a third-year) and Alicia DeMicco (then a senior). Note that a *third-year* co-ran a national-championship team — this is an organisation where responsibility arrives early.
- **Team size:** roughly 30 named members on the 2025 canoe team.

**Why the credential is real:** concrete canoe and steel bridge are not trivia. They require mix design, structural analysis, fabrication, load testing, a formal design paper, and an oral defence — the same deliverable set as a professional bid. Structural and construction firms know exactly what these competitions are, because their senior engineers competed in them.

**Access:** through the UF ASCE student chapter. Recruitment runs on the semester boundary; the build cycle means joining in the fall is materially better than joining in the spring.

> **Focus verdict:** If you are in civil, structural, environmental, or coastal engineering, this is not one option among many. It is the single highest-signal thing you can do with your time at UF, and it outranks most research placements for industry hiring specifically.

### 3.2 SubjuGator & the Machine Intelligence Laboratory — *the club that is also a research lab*

SubjuGator is an autonomous underwater vehicle built by students in the **Machine Intelligence Laboratory (MIL)** — which the research map lists as one of CISE's flagship labs (§3.4). This is the rare organisation that gives you a competition team *and* a lab affiliation at the same time.

**The record:** UF has **participated in every RoboSub competition since its inception in 1998**, and SubjuGator won in **2005, 2006 and 2007** ([subjugator.org](http://subjugator.org/?portfolio=uf-robotics-club)). Twenty-eight consecutive years of participation is its own kind of evidence — it means institutional knowledge is being transferred, not rebuilt from scratch each year.

**Structure:** the team draws from **ECE, MAE, and CISE simultaneously** — one of very few places at UF where undergraduates from three departments work the same problem. MIL's sister vehicle **NaviGator** (autonomous surface vessel) competed at the **2022 Maritime RobotX Challenge** ([mae.ufl.edu](https://mae.ufl.edu/2023/07/07/as-subjugator-prepares-for-robosub-2023-a-look-back-at-navigators-trip-to-the-2022-maritime-robotx-challenge/)).

**Why it matters more than it looks:** autonomy work — perception, state estimation, control, sensor fusion — on a physical vehicle that must not sink is exactly the experience that autonomy employers screen for and that coursework cannot provide. The RoboSub technical design reports are public, which means your work is citable.

> **Focus verdict:** The best single option at UF for robotics and autonomy, and the cleanest way to get a research-lab affiliation through a club door rather than a cold email. Pairs directly with the research map's Tier 1 hardware-security and HiPerGator entries.

### 3.3 Solar Gators — *a genuine national result and a live upward trajectory*

**The record, in order:**
- **2023:** **First place** at the Electrek Formula Sun Grand Prix. Their vehicle *Sunrider* completed the most laps in the field — 707.5 miles on sunlight ([news.ece.ufl.edu](https://news.ece.ufl.edu/2023/07/06/solar-gators-take-first-place-at-2023-formula-sun-grand-prix/)).
- **2024:** fourth at FSGP, and — **for the first time in team history** — qualified for the **American Solar Challenge**, a 1,500+ mile rally from Nashville, Tennessee to Casper, Wyoming. They completed **1,550 miles over eight days** and won the **$10,000 Altair Challenge grand prize** ([ece.ufl.edu](https://ece.ufl.edu/2024/09/13/solar-gators-asc-2024/)).
- **2025:** fifth overall at FSGP, plus the **fastest lap award**, a **$500 MathWorks award**, and **second in the Altair Challenge** ([The Alligator](https://www.alligator.org/article/2025/07/solar-gators-wins-awards-in-formula-sun-grand-prix)).

**Read that trajectory honestly.** A win, then fourth, then fifth is not a decline story — qualifying for and finishing the American Solar Challenge is a considerably harder achievement than winning a track event, and the simulation awards (Altair, MathWorks) indicate the engineering analysis is strong independent of race-day luck.

**Access:** the team publishes an annual information packet through MAE. Recruitment runs at the start of each academic year.

> **Focus verdict:** Battery systems, power electronics, composites, aero, and telemetry, on a vehicle that has to survive 1,500 miles. The single best match at UF for anyone targeting the EV, battery, or renewable-energy sector.

### 3.4 SHPE UF and SASE UF — *the two chapters with national distinctions*

Most professional-society chapters are indistinguishable from one another. These two are not, and both have third-party evidence.

**SHPE UF** received the **Gold Chapter Award at the 2025 SHPE National Convention** in Philadelphia — **one of only four chapters in the nation**, and the **second consecutive year** UF earned it. The award recognises "excellence in leadership, programming and impact within the Hispanic STEM community" ([eng.ufl.edu](https://www.eng.ufl.edu/news/engineering-education/for-the-second-consecutive-year-shpe-uf-takes-the-gold/)). Site: [shpeuf.com](https://www.shpeuf.com/).

**SASE UF** was named **one of two Overall Strongest Chapters out of 86 national chapters** in 2024, plus **UF Student Organization of the Year for 2023–24** and a Life-Long Learner Excellence in Career Readiness award. The chapter has roughly **800 members**, founded 2010, and runs internship and mentorship programs for first-years alongside one of the largest intramural programs on campus ([eng.ufl.edu](https://www.eng.ufl.edu/news/honors-awards/an-everything-organization/)). Site: [ufsase.com](https://ufsase.com/about).

**Why this is worth caring about, unsentimentally:** national professional societies run their own career fairs and conferences, and the sponsoring companies recruit there with far better ratios than at a general campus fair. A Gold Chapter is a chapter whose members get sent to those conventions. That is the mechanism — not the plaque.

**Neither is restricted by ethnicity.** Both are open organisations; the missions are about advancing a community, not gatekeeping membership.

### 3.5 UF iGEM — *a gold medal, and a door whose hinges are undocumented*

UF's iGEM team received a **Gold Medal in October 2024** for **SEPSYNTH — bone marrow organoids as a model to study sepsis** ([cur.aa.ufl.edu](https://cur.aa.ufl.edu/programs-the-international-genetically-engineered-machine/)). iGEM is a genuinely international competition: 300+ teams from 40+ countries.

The team's public work also shows a serious human-practices and outreach component: a synthetic biology and genome-editing lesson plan built for 4th–7th graders and delivered through **UF GEMS (Girls Engaged in Math and Science)**, plus structured stakeholder interviews with clinicians, academic and industry researchers, policy researchers, bioethicists, and educators ([2024.igem.wiki/uflorida](https://2024.igem.wiki/uflorida/education)).

**The honest problem:** UF's Center for Undergraduate Research page for iGEM **does not publish how to join, when applications open, who the faculty advisors are, whether it carries course credit, or how it is funded.** For a gold-medal team affiliated with CUR, that is a real gap. Email CUR directly ([cur.aa.ufl.edu](https://cur.aa.ufl.edu/)) or ask at 202 Newell Hall.

> **Focus verdict:** The strongest wet-lab club credential at UF, and the natural companion to the research map's Tier 1 evolutionary biology and drug discovery entries. Note that the research map's Strategy A recommended pairing the Florida Museum cluster with iGEM — this document confirms iGEM is real and decorated, and also that its intake process is undocumented. Start asking early.

## 4. Deep Dives — Tier 2 Highlights

### 4.1 The two rocket teams, and why there are two

UF has **two separate rocketry organisations** and they are not redundant:

- **Swamp Launch Rocket Team** — solid-motor high-power rocketry, competing in **NASA's University Student Launch Initiative (USLI)** and the **Intercollegiate Rocket Engineering Competition (IREC)**. The team runs **two parallel sets of subteams**, one per competition ([swamplaunch.org](https://www.swamplaunch.org/)).
- **Florida Rocket Lab** — **liquid propulsion**: designing, building and testing liquid propellant rocket engines and flight vehicles. This is substantially harder and rarer at the undergraduate level ([floridarocketlab.org](https://floridarocketlab.org/)). Contact: ufliquidpropulsion@gmail.com.

**Swamp Launch has the most explicitly welcoming onboarding of any team in this document.** From their own join page: recruitment at the **start of Fall and Spring semesters**, **no prior experience necessary**, and — the line worth quoting — *"most of our leads had almost no rocketry experience prior to joining."* New members attend General Education Meetings covering rocketry fundamentals before picking a subteam. Coordination runs through Slack ([swamplaunch.org/join](https://www.swamplaunch.org/join.html)).

If you want propulsion specifically and you are willing to work harder for it, Florida Rocket Lab is the more differentiated credential. If you want to learn how a technical team functions and you are starting from zero, Swamp Launch is the lowest-friction serious door at UF.

### 4.2 Gator Motorsports — Formula SAE Electric

UF's FSAE team **transitioned from combustion to electric in 2022** and now competes against roughly **120 teams from around the world** at the FSAE Electric competition in Michigan each spring ([WUFT](https://www.wuft.org/sports/2023-05-03/gator-motorsports-team-transitions-to-an-electric-car-for-the-formula-sae-competition-in-june)). The F26 car had a public unveiling through the college ([eng.ufl.edu](https://www.eng.ufl.edu/news-events/events/gator-motorsports-uf-fsae-unveiling-of-f26/)). Based in MAE-C; [gatormotorsports.org](https://gatormotorsports.org/); uffsae@gmail.com.

Formula SAE is the most recognised undergraduate engineering competition in the world among automotive and motorsport employers, and the electric transition means the skills now transfer to the EV sector rather than to a shrinking one. Recent placement results could not be verified from a primary source — see flags.

### 4.3 SwampHacks — the flagship hackathon

**SwampHacks XI runs 23–25 January at Newell Hall** ([2026.swamphacks.com](https://2026.swamphacks.com/)). It is UF's flagship hackathon, bringing **300+ students together for 36 hours**. Three distinct entry points, which is worth knowing: **register to hack, register to mentor, or sign up to volunteer.** Mentoring and organising are underrated — they are how you meet the sponsoring companies rather than competing for their attention.

There is also **WiNGHacks**, listed by the college as an explicitly "inclusive, beginner-friendly hackathon environment" ([winghacks.com](https://www.winghacks.com/)) — the right first hackathon if a 36-hour competitive event sounds unappealing.

Note the honest framing: a hackathon win is a weak credential on its own. What hackathons are genuinely good for is **shipping something end-to-end under a hard deadline with people you did not choose** — which is the skill, and it is the thing interviews probe.

### 4.4 Open Source Club — the best CS credential UF clubs offer

Founded **Spring 2016**, and a **partnered Special Interest Group of the UF ACM chapter**. Each semester members collaborate on open source projects, with technical talks alongside ([ufosc.github.io](https://ufosc.github.io/), [docs.ufosc.org](https://docs.ufosc.org/)). All projects live on the [ufosc GitHub organisation](https://github.com/ufosc) under open licences.

**Why this matters more than a typical CS club:** it produces a **public, permanent, attributable commit history**. Given the research map's finding that UF's core CS bench will not carry your letters (§2, Tier 3), a visible open-source record is one of the few things that routes around department reputation entirely — GitHub history is evaluated on its own merits.

### 4.5 Dream Team Engineering — clinical engineering inside UF Health

Students building devices with physicians, nurses and child-life specialists at **UF Health Shands** ([dreamteameng.org](https://www.dreamteameng.org/)). Three branches: a **design branch** (3D modelling for patient-education devices), **Dream Team Software Engineering (DTSE)**, and a **research branch** testing efficacy of devices in actual hospital use. Projects include surgical training models for residents, a model MRI to prepare patients for scanning, and a model Berlin Heart. Founded by Rani Mahmoudi under **Dr. Stephen Hugo Arce**.

This is one of very few undergraduate organisations anywhere with a genuine clinical deployment path — the devices go into a hospital and get used. For anyone on a medical-device, MD, or MD-PhD track, that is a materially different story to tell than a coursework project.

## 5. The Discovery Problem — There Is No Complete List

This is worth stating plainly because it will otherwise cost you time.

**No single page at UF lists all STEM organisations.** The college-wide engineering list omits ASCE, NSBE, SHPE, SASE, ASME, AIAA, BMES, SubjuGator, and Swamp Launch — all of which are real and several of which are the strongest teams in this document. They live on **department** pages instead: MAE has its own list, BME has its own list, math and chemistry each have their own. GatorConnect has everything but is optimised for browsing, not evaluation.

**The practical search order:**
1. **Your department's own organisations page** — this is where the design teams actually are.
2. **[The college-wide engineering list](https://www.eng.ufl.edu/undergraduate/programs-and-partnerships/center-for-experiential-learning/student-organizations/list-of-student-organizations/)** — best for professional chapters and contact emails.
3. **[GatorConnect](https://orgs.studentinvolvement.ufl.edu/organizations)** — the complete 1,000+ index, for anything the first two missed.
4. **The Student Organization Fair** — for everything that has no web presence at all, which is a large fraction.

A stale **2016 HWCOE PDF** still surfaces in search results ([direct link](https://www.eng.ufl.edu/undergraduate/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2016/08/HWCOE-Student-Organizations-and-Design-Teams.pdf)). Do not use it — it is a decade old.

**The pattern worth extracting:** clubs that produce a **physical artifact with a public result** (Steel Bridge, Solar Gators, SubjuGator, rocketry, iGEM) carry real weight with both employers and admissions committees. Clubs that produce a **public record** (Open Source Club) route around institutional reputation. Clubs that produce a **network** (SHPE, SASE, NSBE, SWE) pay off through convention recruiting, not through membership itself. Clubs that produce **only membership** are the ones to be honest with yourself about.

## 7. How to Actually Do This

1. **One serious technical team. One professional chapter. That's it.** The most common failure is joining six organisations, contributing meaningfully to none, and having nothing to say in an interview. Depth in one team beats breadth across five, and it is not close.
2. **Pick the team whose artifact you would enjoy failing at.** Every one of these teams will have a build that does not work three days before a deadline. If that prospect sounds interesting rather than miserable for a given team, that is the one.
3. **Join in the fall, not the spring.** Competition teams run an annual design cycle. Joining in August means you see a full cycle and can hold a subteam lead the following year; joining in January often means arriving mid-build with nothing scoped for you.
4. **Leadership arrives absurdly early if you show up consistently.** A third-year co-managed a national-championship team in 2025. These organisations are chronically short of people who reliably do what they said they would — which is a much lower bar than it sounds and a much rarer one than you would expect.
5. **Use the professional chapter for the convention, not the meetings.** SHPE and SASE national conventions are where the recruiting actually happens. Ask the chapter in your *first* semester how travel funding works, because it is usually allocated on a deadline you will otherwise miss.
6. **Clubs and labs are complements, not substitutes.** The research map's tactical playbook (§5) still applies: 7–10 target PIs, read their papers, email at the semester boundary. A design team gives you something concrete to put in that email — "I built the power distribution board for our solar car" is a far stronger opening than "I am interested in your research."
7. **If nothing here fits, the fair is still worth two hours.** Roughly a third of UF's STEM organisations have essentially no web presence. They are not necessarily worse; they are just invisible from outside.

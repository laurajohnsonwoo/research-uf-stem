UF's elite pockets are narrow, specific, and open to undergraduates.

UF spends $1.33 billion a year on research across 16 colleges, so almost every field has someone doing good work. That fact is useless for deciding where to spend four years. The right question is not “should I do research at UF.” It is “which of the eight genuinely world-class pockets fits me, and how do I get into that specific building.”

Why this map exists

The gap inside UF is bigger than the gap between UF and an Ivy

Choosing a university is the decision everyone agonises over. Choosing a lab is the decision that actually sets the training you get. At UF those two decisions are not remotely the same size.

A student in the Florida Museum's Soltis Lab or the LIGO instrumentation group is training in a top-3-in-the-world environment. A student doing generic machine-learning coursework research at UF is training in a roughly top-60 environment. Same tuition, same transcript, same campus. This map exists to tell those two situations apart.

What four years in this specific building is worth

Standing and access are only half the decision. The other half is what the credential does for you afterwards — which graduate programs it genuinely opens, and which employers hire for the exact thing you learned.

Read this first · inferred, not sourced The research map documents standing and access. Everything below is reasoning from the evidence in the dossiers: who writes your letters, which collaborations already exist, which techniques are scarce, and which sectors currently hire for them. Named programs and employers are illustrative of a category, not placement records.

# 03 · Evidence discipline

The rule that makes the rest defensible.

> **Every substantive claim is either sourced or flagged. There is no third category.**

A map that quietly blends "we verified this" with "we reckon" is worth nothing, because the reader
cannot tell which parts to act on. Making the distinction *visible* is the single highest-value
design decision in this method.

## The flag taxonomy

Six kinds, each phrased differently because they mean different things:

| Flag | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-published claim** | The institution asserts it about itself with no external source or stated methodology | "No. 2 in the country in neuroscience," on the institute's own site |
| **Unverified** | Plausible, could not be confirmed from a primary source | An invention award mentioned only in secondary coverage |
| **Could not re-confirm** | Confirmed on a first pass, not reproducible on a second | An academy election *year* sourced once and never again |
| **Refuted** | Actively checked and found wrong. Correct it and say so. | A paper attributed to the wrong author |
| **Absence of evidence** | Searched and found nothing — which is not the same as confirmed absence | "No ICPC team surfaced at all" |
| **Extrapolated** | A date or figure inferred from the last published cycle | Every next-year deadline |

The distinction between the last two and the others matters. **"We could not find it" is not
"it does not exist,"** and writing it the second way is the most common way this method fails.

## Render the uncertainty, do not footnote it

A flags section at the bottom is where honesty goes to be ignored. Put it in the artefact:

- **Solid** stroke, filled node → documented
- **Dashed** stroke → inferred, no data behind it
- **Hollow**, no connector → no tie exists at all
- **Dashed underline** on a date → extrapolated
- **An inline marker** on any flagged sentence, clickable, with the reason
- **A global toggle** that lights every flagged claim at once

A reader should be able to tell at a glance which parts you would defend.

## Absences are content

No institution publishes what it lacks, so a map that omits gaps is a brochure with better
typography. Every absence should name **the place that has what this one does not** — that converts
a negative into navigation:

> UF has no ICPC presence. UCF placed 1st in North America and 10th worldwide.

That is more useful than any positive claim on the same page, and only an outside map can say it.

## The adversarial second pass

After the research is written, take the load-bearing claims — the ones a decision would hang on —
and try to **refute** them from independent sources. Not re-confirm: refute. Publish the result,
including what survived, what did not, and what you ran out of budget to check.

In the UF pass, 25 claims went in: most confirmed, one refuted and corrected, three could not be
re-confirmed. Publishing that arithmetic is what makes the other 500 claims credible.

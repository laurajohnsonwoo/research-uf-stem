# 01 · The standing rubric

How to decide whether a research area is genuinely world-class, merely solid, or oversold.

The temptation is to reach for rankings. Resist it: rankings are institution-level, lag by years,
and measure output volume, which is exactly what a well-funded mediocre department produces a lot of.

## Tier 1 — genuinely world-class

Requires **at least one** of these, verifiable from outside the institution:

- **Sitting academy-member density in one unit.** Not one member across a college — a cluster in a
  department. Three sitting NAS members in one UF department is the single hardest piece of
  evidence in the whole UF map.
- **Prize density.** Major field prizes — Fields, Turing, Herty, Buckley, Sakurai. Not internal
  "Teacher of the Year" awards, not "40 under 40".
- **A facility that is genuinely rare.** An operating research reactor. 40 million specimens. The
  test: could a peer institution replicate it with money alone? If yes, it is not Tier 1 evidence.
- **A lead role on a national programme.** *Lead*, not partner. UF co-leads IoT4Ag — Tier 1
  evidence. UF is one of five PIs on an NSF AI Institute **led by Delaware** — not Tier 1 evidence,
  and the distinction is the whole point.

## Explicitly insufficient

Each of these looks like evidence and is not:

| Not evidence | Why |
|---|---|
| Rankings alone | Institution-level, lagging, output-weighted |
| Funding volume | Measures size, not standing |
| Faculty headcount | Same |
| A self-published claim | "No. 2 in the country" on the department's own site with no methodology |
| Press-release density | Correlates with communications budget |
| A partner-branded ranking | An assessment firm scoring public disclosures, or a hardware vendor's blog |

## Tier 2 — strong, nationally respectable

Well-funded, real work, good training — **and you would have peers at fifty other universities**.
That last clause is the definition. Tier 2 is not an insult; most excellent departments are Tier 2.

## Tier 3 — the marketing outruns the substance

Real money and real press releases sitting on thinner underlying standing.

**The test:** strip out every source published by the institution itself. Does the claim survive?
If the entire case rests on the institution's own newsroom, it is Tier 3 until proven otherwise.

Tier 3 is the most valuable and most contestable content you will write. Do not soften it, and do
not write it without the external evidence in hand.

## Three modifiers that override the tier

- **Emeritus, retired or departed.** A Fields Medal attached to an emeritus professor is a
  historical honour, not a lab you can join. Record it, and say so in the same sentence.
- **The asset is elsewhere.** UF's connection to the National High Magnetic Field Laboratory is
  real; the magnets are at FSU. Say where the thing actually is.
- **A single person, not a department.** One nationally distinctive researcher in an otherwise
  ordinary department is a person-level bet. Tier the person, not the department, and say which.

## Write the verdict as a decision

Every tiered entry ends with a **focus verdict** — one paragraph that tells a student what to do,
not what to think. "Highest excellence-to-access ratio at the university" is a decision.
"A strong department with many accomplished faculty" is a brochure.
